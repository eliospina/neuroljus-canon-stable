# Robotics for those who need care most (founder stance)

**Date:** 2026-07-29  
**Author context:** Elizabeth Ospina  
**Status:** collaboration compass for assistive care robotics (not a medical claim)

---

## Century horizon (founder contentment)

Robotization of care will arrive someday. The timeline may be a decade, a generation, or — honestly — **a 200-year arc**.

Elizabeth’s measure of success is not speed of deployment. It is this:

> Even if the plan takes two centuries, she would be content if autistic people are the **center** of the care system that is **for them**.

That is the civilizational north star of Neuroljus:

- Build the **person-first protocol contract now** (`care_command_protocol_v0`, witness notes, safety exceptions, human override).  
- So whenever machines enter the room — next year or next century — they inherit **dignity for the beneficiary**, not the neurotypical workplace script.  
- Contentment = the system finally belongs to those it claims to serve.

Neuroljus does not promise 200-year roadmaps as product marketing. It **does** refuse short-term robotics that recenters staff convenience and calls that “care innovation.”

---

## The invitation

Elizabeth wants Neuroljus to **collaborate with the robotization of care services** — directed at those who need it most: **autistic people**, especially those who do not speak neurotypical scripts.

This is not robotics for staff dashboards.  
This is not a toy demo.  
This is **assistive infrastructure** so a person who cannot perform social doublespeak still receives continuity, space, sensory mercy, and human override.

---

## How Elizabeth names the people we serve

Lived stance (moral and observational — **not** a clinical ranking of humanity):

- Many autistic people she has accompanied are **without double agenda**: they do not live the institutional script of appearance, politics, and performance that neurotypical workplaces reward.  
- Their pain and sensory load are often **under-seen and under-relieved** — caregivers who watch long enough know the body can carry more than staff schedules admit.  
- They are not props in someone else’s career story. They are the reason the protocol exists.

Neuroljus product language must stay humble:

- Pain research increasingly challenges the “indifference” myth and documents heightened sensitivity in several measures (see `docs/research/pain-in-autism-bibliography-v0.md`) — cite papers; do not overclaim a single universal law.  
- Do **claim** that pain and discomfort are frequently **missed**, and that care systems must stop ranking the person below staff convenience.  
- Do **claim** that robotics, whenever it arrives, must keep autistic people at the **center** of a care system **for them**.

---

## What robotization may inherit from Neuroljus

```
witness → caregiver judgment → care_command_protocol_v0 → adapter (human | home | robot | wearable)
```

Rules for any robotics partner:

1. **Beneficiary:** the autistic / neurodivergent person in the room — not shift KPIs.  
2. **Authority:** human caregiver override always; `rejection_signal` and `caregiver_pause` are hard stops.  
3. **No mind-reading:** robots execute preauthorized steps; they do not “understand autism.”  
4. **No staff-first optimization:** distance, light, sound, and pause come from person-centered targets.  
5. **Audit:** every run leaves a local trail a substitute caregiver or inspector can read.  
6. **Violence / neglect:** never encode rough handling or waiting-out self-harm as robot “techniques.”

---

## Collaboration posture

Neuroljus is open to research and industry partners in assistive robotics, smart home, and care infrastructure **who accept this contract**.

Partners who want robots to make neurotypical staff faster while the person eats from a soiled floor are not partners.

Partners who want robots to hold soft space, lower demand, notify a trusted human, and preserve dignity — **welcome**.

---

## Closing

Robotization of care will happen with or without ethics.

Elizabeth’s hope — whether in ten years or two hundred — is that it happens **with** the people who do not live other people’s scripts at the **center** of a care system built **for them**.

That contentment is enough. Speed is secondary.
